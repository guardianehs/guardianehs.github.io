§1 — What Guardian is

Designed around how safety is administered on site.

Guardian addresses the four records an EHS function is accountable for — permits, audits, the daily register and the documents that evidence them — with an AI assistant available across all of them. It was developed by a practising EHS professional around the demands of a working day: a permit that must be verified before work begins, audit findings that require owners and closure dates, a register that must reconcile at month end, and the technical questions that arise continuously on site.

The platform runs entirely in the browser. There is no installation and no server to procure. It operates on a laptop in the site office or a handset at the work front, with or without connectivity. Security is treated as a foundation, not an afterthought: every record is isolated by organisation at the database level, administrative access is PIN-gated and rate-limited, and the operator console is deployed separately from the application itself so a single point of failure cannot expose both.

Security by design

Row-level database policies mean an organisation only ever sees its own records — not a permission you configure, a boundary the database itself enforces. No security claim is absolute; this is the architecture, not a promise.

Offline capability

Installs to the home screen and launches without a connection. Permits, audits, the register and the reference engine remain fully available.

Data ownership

No data is transmitted by default. Where an organisation connects its own database, records remain in that account and are protected by row-level security.

§2 — The AI model

Guardian is not a model. It is the domain layer around one.

This distinction is stated plainly because it matters: there is no proprietary "Guardian model" trained on privileged safety data. What Guardian provides is the domain layer — the prompting, the standards context, the permit and audit structure, and a deterministic engine that answers with no model at all. The organisation decides which intelligence sits behind it.

Built-in engine, no connection required

A reference engine covering fifty EHS topics with deterministic calculators: 5×5 risk scoring, LTIFR and TRIR derived from your own man-hours, gas readings assessed against acceptance limits, and threshold rules for excavation depth, working height, noise and manual handling.

Bring your own model

Connect Claude, any OpenAI-compatible API — including no-cost tiers such as OpenRouter or Groq — or a local Ollama model running on your own hardware. Presets complete the configuration in a single step.

Credentials remain local

API keys are stored on the device and transmitted only to the provider you select. No traffic is routed through a Guardian server, as none exists.

Planned development. The EHS assistant is the first model on the platform rather than the last. Project management follows — programme risk, resourcing and schedule questions held alongside the safety record — and thereafter a cyber-security assistant for organisations whose site systems and OT networks require comparable discipline. Each will operate under the same permissions model, the same offline behaviour and the same database.

§3 — Functions

Capabilities, in the order a site uses them.

Permit to work

General, work-at-height, hot work and demolition checklists with Yes/No/N-A responses, PPE selection, gas testing, task risk assessment and isolation references, a SIMOPS check and supporting attachments. A rule-based review returns a determination — authorise, authorise with conditions, or do not authorise — with each blocking item identified. Prints ready for signature.

Audits

Select the audit basis — general, ISO 45001, ISO 14001, ISO 9001, the Factories Act, OSHA or a client standard — then record findings as non-conformity, observation or opportunity for improvement, each with an action, owner and due date. Attach evidence as PDF, Excel, Word or photographs, and issue the report.

Daily register & KPIs

Record the day — manpower, man-hours, inductions, toolbox talks, training, audits, inspections, site walks, quality reports, observations, near misses, first-aid cases and permits — and review it against cumulative totals and a fourteen-day trend. Issue the daily report, or export the register as CSV for analysis.

Documents

Standard templates for immediate use: JSA/HIRA, near-miss and incident reports, toolbox talk records, first-aid and inspection checklists — together with project-level documents including an EHS closure and KPI report, HSE plan, monthly KPI report, internal audit report and emergency response plan.

Duties & approvals

Defined duties rather than nominal settings: a requester raises the permit, a subject-matter expert records review comments, a verifier approves and issues it, and an administrator manages personnel and organisations. Every permission is enforced in the database, not concealed in the interface.

Live across the organisation

Where an organisation is connected, a permit decision reaches the requester as it is taken, and a register entry saved by a colleague is present when the record is opened — without refreshing or re-sending.

Offline operation

Installed to the home screen, Guardian launches without connectivity. The reference engine, calculators, permit review, audit form and register all execute on the device; a connection is required only for a live model or organisation synchronisation.

§4 — Use cases

Where it is used, and what changes.

SettingWhat changes
Integrated facilities management

A single system across every managed site and every client account, so a portfolio-scale EHS team sees the same permit workflow, audit register and reporting structure wherever a job is running.

Global energy, industrial & technology majors

Contractor-heavy sites with hot work, confined space and high-risk lifting run on the same rule-based authorisation chain, with the daily record feeding the KPI reporting these organisations already run internally.

Construction & fit-out projects

Permits progress through request, review and issue with a record of who authorised what. The daily register informs the monthly KPI report and the closure report at handover.

Factories & plants

Hot work and confined-space permits carry gas readings assessed against acceptance limits. Audits are mapped to ISO 45001 or the Factories Act, with findings tracked to closure.

Contractors & vendors

A common standard for permits and toolbox talks across crews, with evidence retained against each job rather than dispersed across personal devices.

NGO & worker-safety programmes

Reduced-rate access is considered case by case — get in touch — with printable material for field teams delivering training where connectivity is unreliable.

Consultants & auditors

A portable audit form with a findings register and an issued report, applying a consistent structure across every client site.

Students & new EHS professionals

A working reference with the calculators, and authentic document formats to learn from rather than theory alone.

§5 — Development

Engineered to be dependable, deliberately lightweight.

Guardian is a lightweight front end with an optional managed database behind it. That architecture is what allows it to run from a handset, a laptop, or a private server inside your own network — and what allows it to keep operating when connectivity is lost mid-shift. Deployments are provisioned per organisation, with data held in an environment you control.

A safety tool that hedges at the moment of decision is worse than no tool, because it launders a doubtful permit into a signed one.

§6 — Documentation

Evaluate in a minute. Deploy an organisation in ten.

Open it

Open the application. All functions are available immediately — permits, audits, register, documents and the offline reference. No account is required.

Install it on the phone

Open Guardian in the browser and select Add to home screen or Install app. It then opens as a native application and launches without a connection.

Connect a model (optional)

In Settings, select the assistant: Claude, an OpenAI-compatible provider, or a local Ollama model. Enter the key and use Test connection. Where none is configured, the offline engine responds.

Provision your organisation

Your organisation's environment is provisioned and connected during onboarding. Personnel are then added against their job titles — EHS officer, permit holder, site engineer, SME, project manager — with each duty enforced at the database level.

Configuration notes, the deployment procedure and administrator access are issued to your organisation during onboarding. Request the deployment pack.

Is Guardian a subscription?

Yes. Guardian is subscription-based for individuals and organisations alike — an Individual plan for a single professional, and an Organisation plan covering a whole team, site or portfolio under one account. Pricing is being finalised; get in touch for current details.

Does it work without internet?

Yes. Once installed, Guardian launches without a connection. The reference engine, calculators, permit review, audit form, register and printing all operate on the device.

Which AI model does it use?

Whichever the organisation selects — Claude, any OpenAI-compatible API including no-cost tiers, or a local Ollama model. Guardian supplies the domain layer around the model, not the model itself.

Where is our data stored?

By default, on the device only. Where an organisation connects a Supabase project, records are held in that account and protected by row-level security, so an individual sees only their organisation's data.

Can it replace our permit system?

Guardian follows the same states as a formal electronic permit system and issues a signature-ready document. Whether it replaces or supplements an existing system is a decision for your management-of-change process; it should be treated as a tool, not an authority.

§7 — About

Built by a practising EHS professional.

Guardian is developed by Arun Williams, co-founder and engineer, an EHS professional based in Bengaluru, India, working in project health, safety and environment, together with co-founder Jobel George and psychologist Sajil KRK, who contributes on human factors in safety. It began as a response to a familiar problem: the knowledge a safety officer relies on is distributed across many standards, the documentation is repetitive, and the available tools are seldom designed around the realities of a work front.

The objective was straightforward: permits that follow a genuine workflow, audits that produce actions rather than adjectives, a register that reconciles, and an assistant that responds with hazards and controls rather than general caution. It started as a free tool because many of the sites that would benefit most have never had a budget for safety software — Guardian is now moving to a subscription so the platform can be maintained and built on properly, while staying within reach of the teams it was built for.

Development is ongoing, and the platform improves fastest with feedback from other practitioners. If any part of it does not reflect how your site operates, please say so.
